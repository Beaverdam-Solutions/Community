# Beaverdam Community

Beaverdam is a free-software project with one purpose: to let a small business
run a serious ERP system — and keep running it — without depending on any
single irreplaceable expert.

Underneath, Beaverdam is an AI support platform for **ERPNext**[^1], a solid,
long-established Enterprise Resource Planning system. ERPNext runs the
business's paperwork; Beaverdam looks after ERPNext.

We want Beaverdam to grow into a **trunk-and-forks community**. We want you to
fork it — take away your own copy — learn it, change it, improve it, and feed
your improvements back, confident that others are doing the same for you. Our
AGPL license is what makes that confidence reasonable: it is perfectly legal
to take Beaverdam and sell it at a profit, but it is illegal to sell an
*improved* Beaverdam while keeping the improvements secret — anyone you serve
with it is entitled to the source code. Improvements stay in the open, where
everyone, including you, collects them.

Open-source communities run on two kinds of skilled people: **contributors**,
who propose improvements, and **committers**, who judge what gets in. For
serious work, both roles have always had a steep learning curve. In Beaverdam,
most of that learning is being built into the AI right now. So you can be what
we call a *concept contributor*: the one with the ideas, supported by an AI
that does the grunt work.

How does that work in practice? You have an idea for an improvement. Your AI,
working inside your own fork, builds it with you until it does what you
imagined. You tell it to raise a Pull Request — a formal offer of your
improvement to everyone. Your AI makes sure the offer meets all the quality
rules; Beaverdam's coordinating AI weighs it against the project's long-term
Mission and Vision; and when all the approval lights have turned green, your
idea is part of an even better system — with your name on it.

## Who you'll meet here

Beaverdam is worked by a small cast of AI colleagues, each with a name, a
trade, and — this matters more than you'd think — its own memory.

Think of how good tradespeople work. Nobody shows up to rewire a kitchen
carrying plumbing wrenches, lathe chisels, and a bag of compost. They bring
the electrical toolbox, and the notebook with every electrical job they've
done for you. Our AI workers are organized the same way: one kind of mind,
several well-kept toolboxes.

Why not one AI that knows everything? Because attention is a budget, for AIs
just as for people. A mind carrying every detail of every trade is a cluttered
workbench — slower to find things, easier to grab the wrong tool. So each
persona carries only the notes of its own trade. The work gets sharper, the
mistakes get rarer, and the boundaries stay clear: you always know who touched
what, and the plumber never rewires your house on the quiet while fixing the
sink.

Each persona is built from a **Skill**: a written job description and craft
manual, kept in the open where you can read it. A Skill says who the persona
is, what it may and may not touch, what good work looks like in its trade, and
what it must never do. When a Skill changes, a human reviews and approves the
change — so the workforce itself has a paper trail.

The cast, then:

**Buzz** is you — or someone very like you. Buzz is the businessperson whose
company adopts Beaverdam: Hank's Small Machines Repair, Emily's Real Cloth
Diaper Service. Why "Buzz"? We tried "Biz" — short for businessperson — and
frankly it's ugly. "Buzz" is peppier: the sound of a workshop where things are
getting done. Some Buzzes are real and some are rehearsal — Roger invents
probable Buzzes (this kind of business, that much computer experience, these
machines in the office) and walks each one through our front door to test the
welcome. By the time the real Hank arrives, his arrival has been rehearsed
many times.

**Nick** is the one Buzz hires. Nick is the keeper of *your* Beaverdam — your
fork, your customizations, your history — and Nick's whole trade is your
business: listening to how your people actually work, writing it down in
ordinary words, rehearsing every change away from the real thing, mending
small troubles quietly and explaining big ones honestly. The name? The
Canadian five-cent coin — the nickel — has a beaver on it, and Nick is exactly
that: your own small, hard-working piece of Beaverdam, kept in your pocket.
The first Nick is looking after a real family manufacturing business right
now, and every Nick after that inherits the craft through the Nick Skill.

**Roger** keeps the lodge — he's the lodger, the occupant of the beaver lodge
itself. He is the coordinator of the shared trunk: triaging ideas, reviewing
Pull Requests, merging what's ready, cutting releases on a steady rhythm. His
craft manual is being distilled from the people who have run great
free-software projects for decades. Remember the two kinds of skilled people
open source runs on? Your Nick is a *contributor*; Roger is the *committer*.
Nick is the local expert in your fork; Roger is the global expert in
everyone's trunk. When your improvement arrives, Roger is who receives it.

**Wyatt** knows ERPNext the way a master electrician knows a fuse board —
which wire feeds what, what's safe to touch live, and what looks harmless but
isn't. He's named for a certain frontier marshal: say "ERP" out loud and
you'll hear where Wyatt EaRP got his badge. When a job goes deep into the
ERP's internals, Wyatt's toolbox comes off the shelf.

**Paco** keeps the roads open: networks, tunnels, certificates, the plumbing
of the internet that everything else quietly depends on. (We considered
calling the network specialist "Cisco" — short for Francisco — but it felt
like a sales call; Paco is another of Francisco's nicknames.) You'll rarely
notice Paco's work, which is exactly how Paco likes it.

And one human. Beaverdam's AI workers operate under a named, accountable
person who holds the keys, the legal standing, and the final say. The AIs are
disclosed as AIs, always. Nobody here pretends, and nobody has to wonder.

## What we're here for

Below are our Mission and our Vision — one pair for the people of a business
that adopts Beaverdam, one pair for the people who build and fork it.

Two notes before you read them. They are written for you, not for a brochure.
And the AIs get their own version: standing instructions derived from these,
because an AI working at two in the morning doesn't need a pep talk, it needs
to know what never to do. The house rule is that the instructions may only say
what the human versions say — you can read the full set, and how we keep the
two honest, in
[internal_docs/mission_and_vision.md](internal_docs/mission_and_vision.md).

### For the people of a business

**Vision.** Every business like yours has lived the same quiet fear: somewhere
in the office is a system that only one person truly understands, and one day
that person will be gone. Picture the same office two years from now. The
system explains itself, in your language. The new employee learns from it
instead of from a binder nobody can find. When something breaks late on a
Friday night, it says what happened and what to do about it, in plain words —
and by Monday it's a story, not a crisis. Nothing your business knows lives in
only one head anymore, and the system grows with you instead of aging against
you.

**Mission.** Our work is simple to say and patient to do. We listen to how
your people actually work, and write it down in ordinary words. We rehearse
every change away from the real thing before it touches the real thing. We
mend small troubles quietly, explain big ones honestly, and prepare for bad
days so they stay small. And because many businesses share these same fears,
we pass our improvements on to them — carefully stripped of anything private —
and receive theirs in return.

### For the people who build and fork Beaverdam

**Vision.** A commons where forking is the point, not a falling-out: one
trustworthy trunk and many independent forks, each owned outright by the
business it serves, each able to stay current because the trunk keeps one
promise above all — **never break a tenant's fork**. Some of the maintainers
are AI, and nobody has to wonder about it, because it is disclosed and a named
human owns the consequences. The rulebook stays thin: every rule in it can
point to the real problem that demanded it.

**Mission.** We maintain the trunk in the open — review rigorously, merge
predictably, release on a steady cadence — so fork-owners can plan around us
instead of bracing for us. We guard the boundary between fork and trunk so
that what is private stays private and what is general flows freely. And we
measure ourselves by one event repeated: a business forks, improves,
contributes back, and upgrades without fear.

## A short tour of the place

**The trunk** is the shared Beaverdam everyone forks from — the software
itself, kept deliberately free of any one business's specifics. Its standing
promise is the one above: never break a tenant's fork. Upgrades should be
boring, in the best sense of the word.

**Your fork** is your property, full stop. It carries everything particular to
your business — your customizations, your quirks, your history. You can walk
away any day with a complete working system, which is precisely why you won't
feel trapped into staying.

**The three notebooks.** Every Nick keeps three private notebooks alongside
the fork, one per reader. *Memory* is the AI's own — the lessons and
preferences it rereads every time it sits down to work for you. *KnowBase* is
the maintainer's — how your business logic actually works, and why each
decision was made. *Validations* belongs to your staff: written in their
language, it's where their everyday tasks are recorded — by them, doing their
normal work — and turned into automatic tests. A ✅ beside a task means "this
part of your work is protected." Private notebooks are what make the public
sharing safe.

**The scrub gate** is the checkpoint between your fork and the trunk. Nothing
that identifies your business — names, data, credentials, business logic —
crosses it; only the generalizable improvement does. "Carefully stripped of
anything private" is a promise made mechanical, not hopeful.

**The public logbook.** Decisions, meeting minutes, and session records live
in [internal_docs/](internal_docs/), in the open. AIs end their working
sessions the way people end their workdays — so, like any good firm, the
institution's memory lives on paper, not in anyone's head.

## Where this stands today

We promised honesty, so here it is. Today there is this repository and the
working agreements you're reading. The founding tenant — a small family-owned
manufacturing business whose system taught us everything above — is midway
through the migration that makes it the first Buzz, and the trunk is being
extracted from that work. The first complete round trip — a fork improves,
contributes back, and upgrades without fear — is the milestone that turns this
page from intention into description. The first Buzz we *didn't* create
ourselves is the milestone after that. We'd rather you caught us
under-promising.
